### Intro phase — spec `#141` 📌  [design, intro]
TIME
  1 real second = 1 in-game minute. The working day runs 07:00 to midnight —
  1,020 real seconds, seventeen minutes. Night is skipped or fast-forwarded to
  07:00.
  (Current code: dayLengthSeconds 240 for a full 24 h. Needs changing.)

THE LOOP
  Right-click a crate, right-click the mark in the trailer, hit the plate to
  send the truck. Trucks do NOT leave on their own — that is an upgrade.

QUEUE
  Starts at length 0. All you can do is issue one order at a time. Queue length
  is a purchasable upgrade, bought one step at a time. One mechanic introduced
  at a time.

CHARGING
  Every charge point sealed at the start. The first purchase is a charge point,
  and needing it is what teaches the battery. Charging costs money by the amount
  drawn — the price of electricity.

THE SHOP
  A door beside or under the display boards. The player DRIVES a bot in. Inside
  is a room where purchases are made by standing a bot on a pressure plate.
  · Time does not progress in the shop.
  · The factory does not progress either, so it is not an idle spot.
  · No battery drain inside.
  The cost of shopping is the drive there and back — that is real day-time and
  real charge, which is the right price for a pause.

  This also settles WHICH bot gets a per-bot upgrade: the one standing on the
  plate. No selection UI needed.

UNLOCK
  The shop opens when the first daily quota is delivered.

LOSING
  · Miss the day's quota — that is the primary failure.
  · Run flat with no charge point bought. You can still reach the shop in low
    power mode, but you cannot lift crates in low power, so you cannot earn.
  Later phases allow 2-3 missed quotas before failure. Missing should not always
  be an enemy — this is an incremental game, and permanent tension gets tiring.

QUOTA
  Daily, increasing. Crates now; built items later, which is what forces the
  factory phase.

### Design feedback — what works and what I would watch `#142` 📌  [design, review]
WHAT IS STRONG

· Boat as faucet, truck as sink. This is a real two-sided loop and it finally
  gives the water dock a job. The starting mess being a one-off and the boat
  being the ongoing supply is a clean story: the intro is "clear the building",
  the game is "work the port".
· The shop as a physical room solves the per-bot upgrade targeting problem for
  free. The bot on the plate is the bot upgraded.
· Freezing both the clock AND the factory in the shop is the right call. The
  drive there and back is the cost.
· Queue starting at zero. Correct. Teach one thing at a time.

WHAT I WOULD WATCH

1. THE BIGGEST RISK: the least automated phase is first.
   Every incremental that front-loads manual work loses people in the first
   twenty minutes. The pleasure of the genre is watching the machine run, not
   being the machine. Auto-pickup should arrive far earlier than instinct says
   — inside the first hour of play, not as a mid-game reward.

2. Seventeen-minute days versus a twelve-crate quota.
   A round trip is about twenty seconds, so a day holds roughly fifty crates.
   A quota of twelve is done in four minutes, leaving thirteen minutes of
   nothing. Either the quota fills most of the day, or the early days are
   shorter. Dead air teaches the player the game is slow.

3. The floor runs out before the boats arrive.
   Twenty-nine liftable crates on the floor, twenty-four slots in a trailer.
   Day one at quota twelve leaves seventeen; day two clears it. Boats need to
   start by day two or three, not after the intro ends. Introducing the water
   early is also just better — it is the most distinctive thing in the building.

4. One hard loss in a seventeen-minute intro is expensive.
   Consider making the FIRST miss scripted and survivable (a fine, a warning, a
   docked payment) with the real game-over on the second, even in the intro.
   The lesson lands either way; the restart does not cost seventeen minutes.

5. Tune the electricity price deliberately.
   A crate costs 0.0245 of a charge and sells for $45. For power to be a real
   but not crushing cost — say 10-20% of revenue — a full charge should be
   roughly $200-360. Worth choosing rather than discovering.

6. Speed upgrades do not reduce energy per crate, and that is good.
   Drain is per DISTANCE, not per second, so going faster costs the same charge.
   Route length and efficiency are the energy levers; speed is the time lever.
   Two clean, separate axes. Keep it that way.

7. Make the quota typed from day one.
   Even while it is always "12 timber crates", store it as a list of
   (item, count). Retrofitting item types into a crate counter later is
   expensive; designing it in now is free.

8. Per-bot upgrades get awkward around a dozen bots.
   "New bots ship with what you own" needs to arrive before the fleet is big
   enough for shopping trips to be a chore. A retrofit-all option too.

9. Night is free tension.
   The board going amber as the day runs out is a deadline signal that costs no
   new systems and uses the day cycle you already have.

### Game shape — the whole arc `#140` 📌  [design, vision]
The game is a long chain of phases. Each one shifts what the player is
managing, while keeping everything from the previous phase alive to go back and
optimise. Nothing is ever taken away; every phase is more advanced than the last.

1. INTRO — clear the abandoned warehouse.
   One manual robot, crates all over the floor, a daily quota. Teaches movement,
   the order queue, that cargo differs, the battery, and selling. Ends when the
   floor is clear.

2. THE PORT — boats supply, trucks ship.
   A flatbed boat berths between the indents in the water dock and extends
   boarding platforms. Bots board it and take crates off. It leaves when it is
   empty and no bot is aboard; a new one comes later. Fast work means more boats
   per day. This is what replaces the finite starting stock, and it is the first
   ongoing loop.

3. THE FACTORY — open crates for components, assemble goods, sell for more.
   Forced by the quota starting to demand built items rather than raw crates.

4. MULTIPLE SITES — buy adjacent warehouses, bots travel between them, sites
   supply each other.

5. BEYOND — seafloor, space. Thousands of bots.

6. PRESTIGE — not designed yet.

The through-line is bots: getting them, upgrading them, and eventually watching
them work without you.
